Key takeaways

  • A flow switch does not measure flow rate. It changes its contacts when flow passes a setpoint.
  • Paddle, piston, shuttle and thermal dispersion designs suit different pipe sizes, pressures and fluids.
  • Gems advises a setpoint at least 25% below the low end of your normal flow, because switch accuracy can be 10 to 20%.
  • Every flow switch has a deadband: it trips on rising flow at one rate and resets on falling flow at a lower one.

A flow switch watches a pipe and changes a contact when flow reaches a set rate. DwyerOmega describes it as a device that makes and breaks an electric current to monitor flow. Gems is clear that it will not show the actual flow rate, only when flow has reached the setpoint. SWME supplies flow switches for plants across the UAE.

How a Flow Switch Works

The flow switch chapter of an industrial instrumentation textbook describes the common paddle design: fluid moving past a paddle pushes it, and the motion actuates one or more switch contacts. The switch is in its normal state for any flow below the trip threshold. Normally open and normally closed describe the contacts in that low flow state.

The Gems liquid flow switch selection guide gives these ranges and says thermal switches need little or no filtration. DwyerOmega's flow switch overview explains the thermal type with two temperature sensors: higher flow cools the heated sensor more, so the temperature difference between them falls.

RS lists further types. Magnetic switches use Faraday's law and need a conductive fluid, which is why they are common in wastewater. Clamp on ultrasonic switches need particles or bubbles in the liquid, so they do not suit drinking or distilled water. In addition, variable area switches use a float in a tapered tube, the same idea as how a rotameter works.

Setpoint and Deadband

Gems recommends finding the average flow rate first and setting the trip point at least 25% below the low end of your flow range. Setting it too close to normal flow causes nuisance trips. Switch accuracy may be 10 to 20%. For example, a 1 GPM setpoint at 20% accuracy may switch anywhere from 0.8 to 1.2 GPM. Gems quotes setpoints from 50 ml/min up to 50 gal/min across its range.

The same textbook gives the deadband example in the diagram: a switch that trips at 15 GPM rising will not reset at 15 GPM falling, but more likely near 14 GPM. Deadband is also called differential. If your setpoint is in GPM or litres per minute, the pipe flow velocity calculator shows what velocity that means in your pipe size.

Did you know?

RS notes that most flow switches made for fire sprinkler systems include a delay timer, so a brief surge in the line does not raise a false alarm.

Where Flow Switches Are Used

Protecting equipment

  • Pumps: stop the motor on loss of supply to prevent dry running (RS).
  • Coolant lines: Gems names loss of coolant flow as the most common use.
  • Chillers: stop on low or no flow that can lead to freeze damage (RS).
  • Boilers: trip on low water flow (RS).

Confirming flow

  • Fire sprinklers: detect water flow above a set rate.
  • Boiler flues: a gas flow switch stops firing until the flue is venting.
  • HVAC ducts: air flow switches trip above or below a set rate.
  • Pool and spa heat pumps: stop when water flow is interrupted.

Selection and Installation

  1. Know the flow range. Find the average flow, then set the trip point below the low end as Gems advises.
  2. Match the fluid. Check wetted parts and body material against the liquid. RS says paddles are sensitive to debris and residue, and industrial paddles avoid magnetic parts in rusty water.
  3. Check temperature and pressure. RS lists high temperature switches rated up to 350 °C and above.
  4. Give it straight pipe. RS suggests a straight section of ideally 10 pipe diameters before and after the switch, away from bends, valves, drains and size changes.
  5. Plan for testing. RS suggests watching the switch change state, or checking continuity across the contacts with an ohmmeter.

If you also need to see the flow rate, a rotameter or a flow meter shows it. Our guide to types of flow meters compares the options, and the flow measurement articles go deeper into each principle.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Setting the trip point near normal flow. Switch accuracy of 10 to 20% puts the trip inside normal operation and causes nuisance trips.
Expecting the switch to reset at the trip point. Allow for the deadband when you write the pump or alarm logic.
Ignoring wear and scale. RS notes that paddles wear and need replacement, and that limescale degrades switches in shower pumps.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a flow switch and a flow meter?

A flow switch only changes a contact at a setpoint. A flow meter measures and shows the flow rate.

Which flow switch has no moving parts?

The thermal dispersion type, which senses the heat that flow carries away from a heated sensor.

Why does my flow switch reset at a lower flow than it trips?

That gap is the deadband, also called differential. The textbook example is a switch that trips at 15 GPM rising and resets near 14 GPM falling.
