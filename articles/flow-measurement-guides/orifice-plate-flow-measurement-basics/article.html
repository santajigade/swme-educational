Key takeaways

  • An orifice plate is a thin plate with a bore that creates a differential pressure; flow is proportional to the square root of that DP.
  • Standard practice keeps the beta ratio, bore over pipe diameter, between 0.20 and 0.75.
  • Permanent pressure loss is often 40 to 80% of the measured DP, and it rises as the beta ratio falls.
  • A good installation needs enough straight pipe, the sharp edge facing upstream and clean impulse lines.

The orifice plate is still one of the most common flow elements in process plants. It has no moving parts and is built to published standards. SWME supplies orifice plates together with DP transmitters and manifolds for complete flow loops.

How Orifice Plate Flow Measurement Works

The plate sits between flanges. As fluid passes through the smaller bore it speeds up and its pressure falls. According to Projectmaterials, flow is worked out from Bernoulli's principle and the continuity equation, using the DP between a tap upstream and a tap downstream of the plate.

A common sizing form is Q = Cd × E × (π/4) × d² × √(2 ΔP / ρ), where E = 1 / √(1 - β4). Projectmaterials notes that sizing follows ISO 5167 Part 2 or AGA Report No. 3. The orifice flow calculator runs this equation, and the flow rate formula guide explains the terms.

Beta Ratio and Pressure Loss

The beta ratio is the bore diameter divided by the pipe inside diameter. ISO 5167 limits it to 0.20 to 0.75 for concentric plates with corner, flange or D and D/2 taps. The Projectmaterials sizing guide gives this trade off:

Beta ratioTypical usePermanent loss (share of DP)
0.20 to 0.40Low flow, high accuracy70 to 90%
0.40 to 0.55Standard55 to 70%
0.55 to 0.65Most common range for process piping40 to 55%
0.65 to 0.75High flow25 to 40%

Because flow follows the square root of DP, ICS Schneider points out that 50% of the DP corresponds to about 70.7% of flow. The DP flow square root calculator does this conversion.

Did you know?

At 10% of full flow an orifice plate produces only 1% of its full scale DP. That square law is why Projectmaterials puts orifice turndown at typically 3:1 to 5:1. Check your own range with the turndown ratio calculator.

Plate and Tap Types

At a glance

Concentric: Centred round bore; Projectmaterials says it is used in more than 90% of installations.
Eccentric and segmental: Offset or segment shaped bore. ICS Schneider describes the eccentric plate as a deliberately asymmetrical special design.
Quadrant edge and conical: Special inlet shapes; Projectmaterials lists quadrant edge plates for Re 500 to 10,000. See the Reynolds number calculator.
Flange taps: 1 inch (25.4 mm) from each plate face; most common in oil and gas and in North America.
Corner taps: At the plate face itself, common in Europe under ISO 5167.
D and D/2 taps: One pipe diameter upstream and half a diameter downstream.

Installation Checklist

  1. Face the edge upstream. The bore edge must be sharp, square and burr free, and the sharp inlet edge must face the flow.
  2. Give it straight pipe. Projectmaterials quotes typically 15D to 44D upstream and 5D to 8D downstream under ISO 5167. Two elbows in different planes may need 40D or more without a flow conditioner.
  3. Do not use a rule of thumb. ICS Schneider warns against applying a general 10D upstream and 5D downstream; take the figure from the standard for your fittings and beta ratio.
  4. Centre the plate. ICS Schneider lists an off centre plate among the causes of error, along with bends, valves and swirl upstream.
  5. Keep impulse lines clean. Gas bubbles in liquid lines, condensate in gas lines and blockages all distort the DP.
  6. Watch for cavitation. ICS Schneider warns of cavitation when local pressure at the bore falls below the vapour pressure of the liquid.

Strengths and Limits

Strengths

  • No moving parts
  • Built to ISO 5167, ASME MFC 3M and AGA Report No. 3
  • Concentric plates cover more than 90% of installations

Limits

  • Turndown typically 3:1 to 5:1
  • Permanent pressure loss of 40 to 80% of the DP
  • Needs long straight runs
  • Edge damage causes error

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Installing the plate backwards. Projectmaterials says this can cause errors above 15%.
Forgetting square root extraction. Without it the system shows 50% flow at 50% DP instead of about 70.7%.
Ignoring worn or damaged bore edges. ICS Schneider names a damaged or eroded edge as a source of measurement error, so inspect the plate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does an orifice plate measure flow?

It creates a pressure drop across a bore; a DP transmitter measures it and flow is calculated from the square root of the DP.

What is a good beta ratio?

Between 0.20 and 0.75. Projectmaterials calls 0.55 to 0.65 the most common range for process piping.

Which side of the plate faces the flow?

The sharp, square edge of the bore faces upstream.
