Key takeaways

  • Fluid spins a bladed rotor at a speed proportional to its velocity, and a pickup turns that rotation into pulses.
  • The K factor is the number of pulses per unit volume; flow rate is the pulse frequency divided by K.
  • Liquid turbine meters are sensitive to viscosity, so a meter calibrated on water reads wrong on a thicker fluid.
  • Omega gives a standard straight run of 10 pipe diameters upstream and 5 downstream, with more after elbows and valves.

How a Turbine Flow Meter Works

Emerson explains that the angular velocity of the rotor is directly proportional to the fluid velocity, and the angle of the blades to the stream sets how fast it turns. A pickoff on the meter body senses the rotor, and its output frequency is proportional to the flow rate. Each pulse also stands for a small fixed volume, so a totaliser can count total flow with a maximum error of one pulse, whatever the volume. The liquid turbine meter overview covers the rotor and pickoff design.

DwyerOmega lists solid state pickups (reluctance, inductance, capacitive and Hall effect) and mechanical gear or magnetic drives. Hall effect sensors work down to 0.2 ft/s, while magnetic pickups need 0.5 to 1.0 ft/s.

The K Factor

Flomec defines the K factor as a calibration constant from the meter manufacturer: the number of pulses per unit volume, for example pulses per gallon or per litre. Omega writes it as K = 60f/Q, with f in Hz, Q in gpm and K in pulses per gallon. Flomec's worked example: 500 Hz × 60 s ÷ 100 pulses per gallon = 300 gpm. The frequency and K factor explainer also shows how to work in litres.

Did you know?

DwyerOmega notes that a K factor applies only to the fluid the meter was calibrated on. Change the fluid and the pulses per gallon change too.

Accuracy and Turndown

At a glance

Accuracy: Typically stated as a percentage of actual rate. Omega gives ±1% of rate or better, and ±0.5% of reading on low viscosity fluids.
Linear range: DwyerOmega says barstock meters are linear to ±0.25% of rate over a 10:1 flow range.
Repeatability: ±0.2% to ±0.02% over the linear range (DwyerOmega); ±0.1% for the Blancett B1100.
Turndown: Up to 100:1 if accuracy is derated to 1% of full scale. Gas meters reach 20:1 at low pressure and up to 100:1 at elevated pressure.
Low end: Meters can show a nonlinear hump in the lower 25 to 30% of their range.

For gas, DwyerOmega notes that meters are designed to make up for the lower driving torque of a low density fluid, and natural gas turbine meters follow AGA Report No. 7. Our guide to types of flow meters shows where turbines sit against other technologies.

Viscosity Effects

Omega calls turbine meters very sensitive to viscosity and viscosity changes. Meters are calibrated on water at 1 centistoke, and a thicker fluid shifts the low end of the range most. DwyerOmega says the meter performs well when the Reynolds number is above 4,000 and up to 20,000, and that temperature effects on viscosity must be compensated or controlled. You can check your line with the Reynolds number calculator.

Omega's remedy is a Universal Viscosity Calibration. NIST turbine meter calibration research plots Strouhal number against Reynolds or Roshko number, and finds the data collapse to a single curve for Reynolds numbers above 2000. NIST avoids the name universal viscosity curve because it does not hold over every part of the curve.

Installation Rules

Upstream fittingStraight run before the meter (Omega)
Standard minimum10 diameters (5 downstream)
Concentric reducer15 diameters
Sweeping elbow or tee20 diameters
Two sweeping elbows25 diameters
Wide open valve14 diameters
Partially open valve50 diameters
  1. Strain the line. Omega gives mesh 100 for 1/4 to 1/2 inch meters, mesh 70 up to 1 1/4 inch and mesh 40 for 1 1/2 to 3 inch. DwyerOmega asks for a strainer or filter at least 20 diameters upstream when solids exceed 100 mg/l of +75 micron size.
  2. Size for the average flow. DwyerOmega suggests an expected average flow of 60 to 75% of maximum capacity, with 3 to 5 psid pressure drop at maximum flow.
  3. Prevent cavitation. Keep downstream pressure at 1.25 times the vapour pressure plus twice the pressure drop.
  4. Fill slowly. The Blancett manual says to open the upstream isolating valve slowly to avoid hydraulic shock.
  5. Watch the bearings. Omega says bearing life is roughly inversely proportional to the square of bearing speed; at 33% of maximum flow, life extends by a factor of ten.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Blowing air or gas through a liquid meter. The Blancett manual warns excess air pressure can damage the rotor and bearings by overspinning.
Using a water K factor on oil. Viscosity shifts the calibration; calibrate on the actual fluid or use a viscosity calibration.
Skipping the straight run. The Blancett manual says accuracy may be affected without 10D upstream and 5D downstream. Our blog on flow meter incorrect readings lists other causes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What output does a turbine flow meter give?

A pulse frequency proportional to flow rate. Counting the pulses gives the total volume.

How do I find the flow from the frequency?

Divide the frequency by the K factor. For gpm with K in pulses per gallon, flow = 60 × f ÷ K.

Why does my turbine meter read wrong at low flow?

The low end is most affected by viscosity and the nonlinear hump in the lower 25 to 30% of the range.
