Key takeaways

  • An interface is the boundary between two liquids that do not mix and have different densities, usually oil above water.
  • In practice an emulsion or rag layer sits between them and blurs the boundary for most technologies.
  • DP and displacers rely on a density difference, guided wave radar and capacitance on a dielectric difference.
  • No single technology covers every interface application, so the emulsion and the accuracy you need decide the choice.

Separators, desalters and settling tanks have to control where oil ends and water begins. Interface level measurement finds that boundary, often together with the total level. SWME supplies level transmitters and level gauges for interface duties across the GCC.

What an Interface Is

According to Automation Forum, an interface is the line between two liquids that do not mix and have different densities. An emulsion, or rag layer, is droplets of one liquid dispersed in the other. It makes density and dielectric change gradually instead of in one step, and that degrades DP, displacer and radar readings. Thick rag layers can form at startup or after a surge in production flow.

Endress+Hauser, writing in Process Online, adds that every extra medium in a vessel brings two interfaces, one on each side of its emulsion layer.

Did you know?

Endress+Hauser counts five interfaces in a vessel holding oil, water, sand and foam.

Endress+Hauser says mechanical systems can cost up to three times more than electronic ones on larger projects. Capacitance gives a stable reading even when there is no sharp interface, is not affected by density or temperature, and can be calibrated without filling the vessel. Guided Wave Radar Limits

Guided wave radar reads total level and interface from one probe. Endress+Hauser sets the boundary conditions below. Our guided wave radar guide covers probe choice and installation.

At a glance

Upper liquid: Dielectric constant known, constant and below 10.
Layer thickness: The upper layer must be more than 60 mm thick.
Dielectric difference: More than 10 between the two liquids.
Emulsion: Less than 50 mm thick. Automation Forum notes thin emulsions below 2 inches may still be detected.
Retrofit: No wet calibration, density independent, and can replace a displacer in its existing chamber.

Selection Checklist

  1. Total level. Decide whether total level is constant or variable, and whether you need it as well as the interface.
  2. Emulsion. Thin emulsion allows radar or GWR; thick emulsion points to nuclear or a hybrid of capacitance and GWR.
  3. Accuracy. Automation Forum gives ±2 mm as a possible need for custody transfer and ±25 mm as enough for control.
  4. Mounting. Displacers and GWR usually need side chambers, and internals can block a free space radar.
  5. Calibration cost. Methods that need wet calibration, filling the vessel with each fluid, cost more over their life.
  6. Safety. A SIL target under IEC 61511 may call for 1oo2 or 2oo3 voting with a primary and backup instrument.

Strengths and Limits

Clean oil and water

  • DP, displacer, GWR and capacitance all suitable
  • Non contact radar rated not suitable
  • Choose on cost, accuracy and mounting

Heavy emulsion

  • DP, displacer, radar and GWR rated not suitable
  • Capacitance and hybrid partly suitable
  • Nuclear rated suitable

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Calibrating at the wrong density. Automation Forum advises calibrating at the real process density.
Ignoring the rag layer. A thick emulsion defeats DP, displacer and radar methods.
Leaving probes dirty. Clean probes regularly and keep stilling wells calm.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is interface level measurement?

Measuring the boundary between two liquids that do not mix, such as oil on water, often together with total level.

Can guided wave radar measure interface?

Yes, when the upper liquid dielectric is below 10, the difference is above 10 and the emulsion is thinner than 50 mm.

What works with thick emulsion?

Nuclear gauges are rated suitable; capacitance and hybrid systems are partly suitable.
