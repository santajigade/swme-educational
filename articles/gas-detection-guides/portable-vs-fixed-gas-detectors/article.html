Key takeaways

  • A fixed gas detector protects a facility and gives early warning of leaks; a portable monitor protects the person wearing it.
  • Fixed systems can trigger alarms, ventilation, process shutdowns or fire suppression; portable units are not tied to any safety system.
  • MSA says most portable monitors detect one to four gases, and advanced models up to six.
  • Both are point detectors that only see gas reaching the sensor, and both need regular gas testing.

A fixed gas detector and a portable monitor do different jobs. MSA calls their roles complementary, and Industrial Scientific says you should never give up a personal monitor because a fixed one is already in the area. Fixed Gas Detector vs Portable Monitor

Industrial Scientific says fixed monitors are used mainly to protect a facility, detect catastrophic leaks or give early warning, while personal monitors protect the people working in an area. MSA describes a fixed system as anything from one detector to hundreds, hardwired to a wall mounted controller or brought into a PLC or DCS. A portable monitor moves with the user and reads the gas in the breathing zone.

For sensor choice, MSA notes infrared sensors are more common in fixed systems because of their higher power needs, and ultrasonic leak detection is also typically fixed. Our guide to types of gas sensors covers each principle.

Alarms and Connections

Interscan's comparison says both types have audible and visual alarms for gas above exposure limits, low oxygen or the LEL. In a fixed system, one sensor in alarm can set off the others on the same control panel. MSA adds that smart detectors with HART or Modbus make integration with a PLC or DCS simpler. For control system layout, see PLC hardware selection and SCADA system architecture.

At a glance

Breathing zone: Interscan says to wear a portable unit no more than 10 inches from the nose and mouth.
Gas behaviour: MSA notes methane tends to rise, hydrogen sulfide tends to sink and carbon monoxide mixes readily with air. See our gas detector placement guide.
Sensor life: MSA puts electrochemical sensor life at commonly two to three years, with newer designs reaching five to seven.

Did you know?

MSA points out that few organisations can justify a fixed gas detector in every manhole or confined space. Portable monitors with sampling pumps and tubing let workers test the space before they enter, without exposing themselves.

Testing and Maintenance

Industrial Scientific says the only way to be certain a monitor will detect gas is to test it with gas. Interscan says both types ideally need a bump test before every shift, and that fixed sensors are less likely to be bump tested and calibrated in practice. Sensidyne says personal monitors must be checked before each use: battery, response to the target gases and alarms. Read more in bump test vs calibration.

Fixed

  • Periodic inspection
  • Calibration at the maker's interval
  • Sensor replacement by service life

Portable

  • Functional check before each use
  • Often bump tested in a docking station
  • Rechargeable battery care

How to Choose

  1. Hazard. A known, continuous leak risk points to a fixed gas detector; changing or temporary work points to portable.
  2. Action needed. If gas must start ventilation or shut a process down, only a fixed system can do it.
  3. People. For confined space entry and maintenance, MSA points to portable monitors, even where fixed detection exists.
  4. Gases. List them; for toxic gas see H2S gas detection and for flammables LEL and UEL.
  5. Area rating. MSA lists intrinsically safe and explosion proof or flameproof designs for hazardous locations.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Dropping personal monitors because fixed detection is installed. Industrial Scientific says a leak and a worker can both end up in a spot no fixed detector covers.
Treating portable monitors as continuous area monitoring. Interscan says they are not made for it.
Choosing on price alone. MSA says the two are not interchangeable on price.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a portable monitor replace a fixed gas detector?

No. MSA says portable detectors supplement fixed systems rather than replace them.

Can a fixed system shut down a process?

Yes. It can be configured to trigger alarms, ventilation, process shutdowns or fire suppression.

How often should I bump test?

Interscan says ideally before every shift, for both fixed and portable detectors.
