Key takeaways

  • A butterfly valve is a quarter turn valve with a disc that rotates on a shaft in the flow stream.
  • The three main butterfly valve types are concentric, double offset and triple offset.
  • Each added offset reduces seat rubbing, which allows harder seats, higher pressure and higher temperature.
  • Body style (wafer, lug or double flanged) decides how the valve bolts into the line and whether it can serve as a line end.

Butterfly valves are light, compact and quick to operate, which makes them a common choice for large lines. But a rubber lined water valve and a metal seated steam valve are very different products. Knowing the main butterfly valve types helps you match the design to the pressure, temperature and shutoff the line needs.

How a Butterfly Valve Works

Fabrico describes the butterfly valve as a quarter turn valve that uses a rotating disc mounted on a shaft through the pipe. In the closed position the disc edge presses against the seat to seal. Because the disc and shaft stay in the flow stream, there is some permanent pressure loss even when the valve is fully open. Operation is by handle, gearbox, pneumatic or electric actuator.

Main parts

Disc: The closing member; it turns 90 degrees from fully closed to fully open.
Shaft: Carries the disc and transmits torque from the operator.
Seat: Resilient (EPDM, NBR or Viton), soft polymer or metal, depending on the type.
Throttling: Fabrico limits throttling to roughly 20 to 70 degrees of disc rotation.

Butterfly Valve Types by Offset

The types are named after where the shaft sits relative to the disc, the seat and the pipe. Project Materials explains the three offsets. In the first, the shaft moves behind the plane of the seat. In the second, it moves off the centreline of the pipe bore, which creates a cam action on closure. In the third, the seating surface is machined to a conical profile, so contact occurs only at final closure.

Did you know?

Actuators are sized on breakaway torque, the torque needed to unseat the disc. Fabrico notes that mid stroke torque is lower, and that resilient seats gain breakaway torque as they age, so size with margin. For flow capacity, use the control valve Kv and Cv calculator.

Body Styles

Wafer

  • Clamped between two pipe flanges
  • Bolts pass through the whole assembly
  • Compact face to face length
  • Cannot isolate one side on its own

Lug

  • Threaded lugs bolt each side to its own flange
  • Suits dead end service
  • Check the dead end rating

Double flanged bodies have integral flanges on both ends and are used in larger sizes or higher pressure service beyond wafer and lug ratings, according to Fabrico.

Selection Checklist

  1. Service. Define fluid, pressure class and maximum temperature first.
  2. Shutoff. Resilient seats give bubble tight shutoff; metal seats need a triple offset for tight closure.
  3. Duty. Isolation or throttling; for control duty see our pneumatic control valves.
  4. Body. Wafer, lug or double flanged to suit the piping and maintenance plan.
  5. Operator. Lever, gearbox or actuator sized on breakaway torque.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using a rubber seated valve in hot hydrocarbon service. Choose a triple offset metal seated design instead.
Fitting a wafer valve at a line end. Use a lug body rated for dead end service.
Throttling near the closed position. Keep control within the usable opening range to protect the seat.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main butterfly valve types?

Concentric (zero offset), double offset (high performance) and triple offset.

Why is it called triple offset?

Two shaft offsets plus a conical seat, so the disc touches the seat only at final closure.

Wafer or lug: which do I need?

A lug body when the valve must hold pressure with one side unbolted; a wafer body otherwise.
