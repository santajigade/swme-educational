Key takeaways

  • A Y strainer is compact and strong, with some models good for 400 bar g, and fits horizontal lines or vertical lines with downward flow.
  • A basket strainer holds more dirt and has a lower pressure drop size for size, but it only goes in horizontal lines.
  • Aim for a screen open area about four times the pipe area, and choose perforation or mesh by the particle size you need to stop.
  • Clean the screen when the differential pressure reaches about 5 to 10 psi; duplex basket strainers let you do it without stopping flow.

A Y strainer and a basket strainer do the same job: they catch scale, weld slag and other debris before it reaches pumps, control valves, meters and steam traps. The difference between a strainer and a filter is mainly particle size, with strainers catching particles larger than about 75 to 100 microns. SWME supplies Y, basket and duplex strainers alongside its range of industrial valves.

How Each Strainer Is Built

Spirax Sarco's guide to pipeline strainers describes the Y type body as a compact cylindrical shape that is very strong and can handle high pressures, often up to 400 bar g. The screen sits in the angled leg, and a blowdown valve can usually be fitted in the cap. A basket strainer has a vertical chamber, typically larger than a Y type, with a removable basket under a cover.

Project Materials' overview of pipeline strainer types notes that a basket strainer holds more debris than a Y strainer of the same size because its element is larger.

Installation and Orientation

Which way round

Y strainer, liquid: In horizontal lines the pocket should point vertically downwards (Spirax).
Y strainer, steam or gas: In horizontal lines the pocket should lie in the horizontal plane (Spirax).
Y strainer, vertical line: Only with downward flow (Spirax, Titan FCI).
Basket strainer: Horizontal lines only (Spirax, Keckley).

Put a strainer upstream of the equipment it protects. Our guide to types of industrial valves covers the valves usually installed around it, and the article on pump protection instruments shows where a strainer fits in a pump suction.

Open Area Ratio

Watts' strainer facts sheet says the open area of the screen or basket should be four times the cross sectional area of the pipe. Its example, a 3 inch Y strainer with 1/8 inch perforations, has 31.4 sq in of open area, a ratio of 4.25 to 1. Keckley's screen options sheet gives the method: screen area times percent open area, divided by the pipe ID area. For a 2 inch Y strainer with 20 mesh that is 17.753 ÷ 3.356 = 5.29 to 1.

Did you know?

Watts calculates that a basket 50% clogged has twice the pressure drop of a clean one. In its example, 0.4 psi at 1,000 GPM of water becomes 0.8 psi.

Choosing the Screen

Screens are perforated plate, mesh, or mesh lined perforated plate. Spirax puts perforated hole sizes typically between 0.8 and 3.2 mm, and says mesh openings as small as 0.07 mm are possible.

When to Clean

Mueller Steam Specialty's Y strainer instruction manual says the screen normally needs cleaning when differential pressure reaches 5 to 10 psi, and that a strainer with a blowdown valve can be flushed by opening it until the sediment is gone. Caleffi's note on strainer maintenance puts the cleaning point at about 5 or 6 psi, warns against letting it go above 10 psi, and suggests recording the clean differential pressure at design flow as a baseline.

A pressure gauge on each side of the strainer shows the loss as it clogs. A single differential pressure gauge across the strainer reads it directly, as our differential pressure gauge guide explains. Duplex and Temporary Strainers

Duplex basket strainer

  • Two baskets with a diverter valve
  • One stays in service while the other is cleaned
  • For lines that cannot be shut down

Temporary strainer

  • Conical, basket or plate type
  • Protects equipment during start up
  • Cone types can collapse under high differential pressure if left in

Selection Checklist

  1. Check the line orientation. Vertical with upward flow rules out both; vertical downward flow rules out a basket strainer.
  2. Estimate the dirt load. Heavy debris favours a basket strainer, which holds more.
  3. Decide if flow can stop for cleaning. If not, choose a duplex basket strainer.
  4. Pick the screen. Match the opening to the particle size the protected equipment can pass.
  5. Check the open area ratio. Aim for about four times the pipe area.
  6. Plan for monitoring. Fit gauges or a differential pressure gauge and set a cleaning point.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Installing a Y strainer pocket down on steam. On steam or gas the pocket should lie horizontal.
Putting a basket strainer in a vertical line. Basket strainers are for horizontal lines only.
Leaving a temporary cone strainer in place. It can collapse under high differential pressure.
Running until the screen is blocked. Pressure drop doubles at 50% clogged; clean at 5 to 10 psi.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has the lower pressure drop?

Size for size, a basket strainer has a lower pressure drop than a Y strainer, according to Spirax Sarco.

Can a Y strainer go in a vertical pipe?

Yes, if the flow is downwards.

What mesh is 840 micron?

20 mesh, with an opening of about 0.034 in.
