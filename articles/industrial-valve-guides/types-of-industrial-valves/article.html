Key takeaways

  • Valves are grouped by what they do: isolate, regulate, prevent backflow or protect against overpressure.
  • Gate, ball and butterfly valves are mainly used for on and off isolation.
  • Globe and control valves are designed for throttling and accurate flow control.
  • Check and safety relief valves work automatically, without an operator.

Every process plant relies on valves to start, stop and control flow. With so many designs on the market, it helps to understand the main types of industrial valves and the job each one is built to do. Choosing a valve for the wrong duty is a common cause of leaks, wear and poor control.

Valve Functions

Before looking at individual designs, decide what the valve must do. Most valves fall into four functional groups.

{{infographic}}

Main Types of Industrial Valves

Gate valves

A flat or wedge shaped gate moves up and down across the flow. When fully open, the bore is clear, so pressure loss is very low. Gate valves are ideal for isolation in large lines, but they should not be used half open, because the gate vibrates and the seats erode. For wellhead and pipeline duty, see API 6A gate valves and API 6D gate valves.

Globe valves

A disc moves onto a seat inside a spherical body, and the flow changes direction through the valve. This shape gives good throttling control and tight shut off, at the cost of higher pressure loss. Globe valves are common on steam, cooling water and bypass lines.

Ball valves

A ball with a bore rotates a quarter turn to open or close. Ball valves operate quickly, seal tightly and suit most gases and liquids. For corrosive chemicals, PTFE and PFA lined ball valves protect the body from attack.

Butterfly valves

A disc rotates a quarter turn inside the pipe. Butterfly valves are light, compact and economical in large diameters, so they are popular in water treatment, cooling and HVAC systems. High performance and triple offset designs extend their use to higher pressures and temperatures.

Check valves

A disc, ball or flap opens with forward flow and closes when flow reverses. Check valves protect pumps and compressors from backflow and need no operator. Swing, lift, dual plate and piston designs suit different flow rates and orientations.

Safety relief valves

A spring holds the valve closed until system pressure reaches the set point. The valve then opens to release fluid and protect equipment and people. Safety relief valves are the last line of defence against overpressure, so they must be sized, set and tested correctly.

Control valves

A control valve receives a signal from the control system and positions its trim to hold a set flow, pressure, level or temperature. Pneumatic control valves and pressure reducing valves are typical examples. Sizing uses the flow coefficient; try the control valve Kv and Cv calculator and the valve authority calculator.

Did you know?

A gate valve used for throttling can wear out in weeks. At small openings, high velocity flow hits the bottom of the gate and the seat, cutting grooves called wire drawing. Once damaged, the valve can no longer seal. Use a globe or control valve wherever flow must be regulated.

Standards and Pressure Ratings

API 600 and API 6D: steel gate valves for refinery service and pipeline valves.
API 6A: wellhead and Christmas tree equipment, rated in psi up to 20,000 psi and above.
ASME B16.34: pressure and temperature ratings for flanged, threaded and welded end valves.
ASME classes: 150, 300, 600, 900, 1500 and 2500, matching the flange rating of the line.
API 526 and API 520: dimensions and sizing for pressure relief valves.

Valves also need clean fluid to seal well. Installing strainers upstream protects seats from scale and weld debris. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Throttling with gate or ball valves. Seats erode and the valve stops sealing.
Wrong check valve orientation. Some designs only work in horizontal or upward flow.
Ignoring pressure class. A class 150 valve on a class 300 line is a serious safety risk.
Oversized control valves. The valve works near closed and control becomes unstable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common type of industrial valve?

Ball and gate valves are the most common, because most valves in a plant are used for simple on and off isolation.

What is the difference between a gate valve and a globe valve?

A gate valve is designed for full open or full closed isolation with low pressure loss, while a globe valve is designed for throttling and flow control.

Which valve prevents backflow?

A check valve, also called a non return valve, closes automatically when flow tries to reverse.
