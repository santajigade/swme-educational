Key takeaways

  • A safety relief valve opens automatically when pressure reaches its set pressure and closes again once pressure falls.
  • Safety valves pop open on gas and steam; relief valves open in proportion to the rise on liquids; a safety relief valve does both.
  • Under ASME Section VIII, accumulation is limited to 10% over MAWP for a single valve, 16% for multiple valves and 21% for fire.
  • Inlet pressure loss should stay within 3% of set pressure, and the bonnet should point vertically upwards.

A safety relief valve protects a pressure vessel, boiler or pipeline from overpressure. Inlet static pressure opens it, so it needs no power or signal. SWME supplies safety relief valves, pressure reducing valves and other industrial valves for plants across the UAE and GCC.

Safety Valve, Relief Valve or Safety Relief Valve

Safety valve: Spirax Sarco describes it as actuated by inlet static pressure, with rapid opening or pop action. It is used on steam and gases.
Relief valve: Opens with a gradual lift that is generally proportional to the overpressure. It is typically used on incompressible fluids such as water and oil.
Safety relief valve: Opens with pop action or in proportion to the pressure rise, so it works as a safety valve on gas and as a relief valve on liquid.

How a Safety Relief Valve Works

A spring holds the disc down on the nozzle. The set point is adjusted by turning the adjusting nut to change the spring compression. When inlet pressure on the disc overcomes the spring force, the disc lifts. Fluid then enters the huddling chamber, the annular space between the nozzle exit and the disc holder. This exposes a larger area, the opening force rises and the valve pops open.

With the larger area still exposed, the valve does not reclose at set pressure. Pressure has to fall below it first, and that gap is the blowdown. The relief and safety valve overview also notes that a lever on safety valves is used as an operational check.

The Emerson pressure relief valve engineering handbook gives the ASME Section VIII accumulation limits shown below. For valves with adjustable blowdown, it says the typical reseat pressure is 7% to 10% below set pressure.

Spirax Sarco gives typical overpressure of 3% to 10% for compressible fluids and 10% to 25% for liquids, and typical blowdown below 10% for gases and up to 20% for liquids. The Kunkle technical reference advises keeping operating pressure at least 5 psig or 10% below set pressure, whichever is greater. Set pressure is a gauge value; the absolute and gauge pressure calculator converts it when a datasheet quotes absolute pressure.

Did you know?

API Standard 527 tests seat tightness at 90% of set pressure. Emerson notes that a soft seated valve must show no leakage at all at that pressure.

Conventional, Balanced Bellows and Pilot Operated Valves

Conventional spring loaded

  • The spring acts directly on the disc
  • Emerson: built up back pressure should not exceed 10% of set pressure at 10% overpressure

Balanced bellows

  • Built for higher back pressure than a conventional valve
  • Emerson: total back pressure up to 30% of set pressure with no capacity loss
  • Limits vary by maker, so check the datasheet

In a pilot operated valve, system pressure sits on top of a piston and holds the main valve shut. When the pilot vents that space, inlet pressure opens the main valve. Installation and Testing

  1. Mount it upright. Spirax Sarco says safety valves should always be installed with the bonnet vertically upwards.
  2. Limit inlet loss. ASME VIII Appendix M limits inlet losses to 3% of set pressure; higher losses cause chatter.
  3. Size the pipes. Inlet and outlet piping must be at least as large as the valve connections.
  4. Slope the discharge. Horizontal runs need a fall of at least 1 in 100 so fluid cannot collect.
  5. Lock block valves open. Any block valve must be car sealed open and logged, and closed only for inspection, test or repair.
  6. Test on a schedule. Emerson notes API 576 intervals may be as long as ten years, but service history should set the schedule.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Running too close to set pressure. Kunkle advises a margin of at least 10% or 5 psig, whichever is greater.
Ignoring back pressure. Emerson limits built up back pressure on a conventional valve to 10% of set pressure; above that, consider a balanced bellows valve.
Long or small inlet pipes. Inlet loss above 3% of set pressure makes the valve chatter.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between overpressure and accumulation?

Overpressure is measured above the set pressure; accumulation is measured above the MAWP of the system.

Why does the valve reseat below set pressure?

Once open, the disc exposes a larger area, so pressure has to fall further before the spring can close it. That gap is blowdown.

Can I fit an isolation valve under a relief valve?

Only where the code allows it, and it must be locked open. ASME Section I allows no isolation valve at all.
