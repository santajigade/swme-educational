Key takeaways

  • The pascal (one newton per square metre) is the SI unit; bar, psi, atm and the liquid column units are all defined against it.

The Pascal and the SI System

NIST Special Publication 811 defines the pascal as one newton per square metre. It is a small unit, so plants use kilopascals and megapascals: 1 kPa is 1000 Pa and 1 MPa is 1 000 000 Pa. The same publication lists the bar (10⁵ Pa) under units in use temporarily with the SI. It says these units should not be introduced where they are not already used.

The NIST guide to SI units also has rules for writing values: a space between number and symbol (150 kPa), no plural s on a symbol, and no full stop after it except at the end of a sentence.

How to Convert Pressure Units

Every factor in the table is a value in pascals, so pascals work as a hub. Multiply your reading by the factor for its unit to get pascals, then divide by the factor for the unit you want.

Worked examples

10 bar to psi: 10 × 100 000 = 1 000 000 Pa; 1 000 000 ÷ 6894.757 = 145.04 psi.
150 psi to bar: 150 × 6894.757 = 1 034 214 Pa; ÷ 100 000 = 10.34 bar.
1 atm to bar: 101 325 ÷ 100 000 = 1.01325 bar, or 14.696 psi.
1 kgf/cm² to bar: 98 066.5 ÷ 100 000 = 0.980665 bar. Close to 1 bar, but not equal.
25 mbar to inH₂O: 25 × 100 = 2500 Pa; ÷ 249.0889 = 10.04 inH₂O.

Did you know?

A kgf/cm² is 0.980665 bar, so the two are about 2% apart. At the top of a 0 to 16 range the gap is about 0.31 bar, so a gauge marked in one cannot stand in for the other.

Liquid Column Units

Inches of water and millimetres of mercury describe pressure as the height of a liquid column, and the liquid's density changes with temperature. Omega labels its factors "inch of water at 4 °C" and "mm of mercury at 0 °C". NIST SP 811 lists the inch of water at 39.2 °F (249.082 Pa) and at 60 °F (248.84 Pa) as separate units. The difference is under 0.1%, which matters only for precise low pressure work such as calibrating a draft gauge.

The hydrostatic pressure formula shows where these units come from: pressure equals density times gravity times height. The manometer calculator gives the pressure difference for a manometer reading in your fluid.

Specifying Units on Instruments

  1. Say gauge or absolute. psi alone does not say whether the reading starts from atmosphere or from vacuum. Omega's altitude table gives 14.70 psia at sea level. Our guide to gauge, absolute and differential pressure explains the three references.
  2. Use one unit per site. Match the unit already on the P&ID and in the DCS so operators do not convert in their heads.
  3. Ask for dual scales where needed. A dial in bar and psi helps when equipment comes from different countries.
  4. State the reference temperature for water columns. Write inH₂O at 4 °C or at 60 °F when the range is small.
  5. Check the transmitter's engineering units. The 4 to 20 mA range must be set in the same unit as the DCS scaling. The absolute and gauge pressure calculator converts between the two references when a datasheet uses the other one.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Treating kgf/cm² as bar. They differ by about 2%, as the worked example above shows.
Leaving out the g or a. A range of 0 to 10 psi could be gauge or absolute; the order must say which.
Rounding the factor too early. Using 14.5 psi per bar instead of 14.504 adds a small error that grows on high ranges.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is bar an SI unit?

No. NIST SP 811 lists it as a unit in use temporarily with the SI. It equals exactly 100 000 Pa.

How many psi are in one bar?

100 000 ÷ 6894.757 gives 14.504 psi per bar.

Why do inches of water have more than one value?

The value depends on the water temperature used as the reference, such as 4 °C (39.2 °F) or 60 °F.
