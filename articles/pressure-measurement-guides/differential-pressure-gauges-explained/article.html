Key takeaways

  • A differential pressure gauge has a high and a low port and shows the difference between the two pressures on one dial.
  • Piston and diaphragm designs are common at ±2% or ±5% of full scale; bellows and Bourdon designs reach ±1/2% or ±1%.
  • In most DP service the working pressure on the ports is far higher than the DP range, so check the maximum working pressure first.
  • Typical jobs are filter and strainer monitoring, flow across an orifice and level in open or closed tanks.

Two ordinary gauges cannot show a small difference sitting on a high line pressure. A differential pressure gauge takes both pressures into one instrument and shows only the difference. SWME supplies differential pressure gauges and gauge accessories for plants across the UAE and GCC.

How a Differential Pressure Gauge Works

The gauge has a high pressure port and a low pressure port. The sensing element sits between them and moves with the difference, and a magnet or linkage turns the pointer. For the difference between gauge, absolute and DP readings, see our guide to gauge vs absolute vs differential pressure.

Sensing Designs

The Midwest Instrument selection guide and NOSHOK describe four main designs.

DesignHow it sensesSuitsStated accuracy
PistonA floating piston and magnet move in a bore against a calibrated spring; a rotary magnet outside drives the pointerLiquids. A little liquid bypasses from high to low port, which Midwest says is often too much for gas±2% or ±5% full scale
DiaphragmAn elastomer diaphragm moves against a range spring and carries an internal magnetDissimilar fluids, air, gases and liquids with a high solids content, because the ports are fully isolated±2% or ±5% full scale
BellowsDP acts on a bellows and linkage that drive the pointerClean service where accuracy matters±1/2% or ±1% full scale
BourdonA Bourdon tube sits inside a pressure chamber and reads the difference between inside and outside, coupled by magnetHigh working pressures±1/2% or ±1% full scale

WIKA leaves bellows out of its DP range because bellows are susceptible to pressure spikes, and recommends diaphragm types instead. Where a 4 to 20 mA signal is needed rather than a local dial, use a differential pressure transmitter.

Working Pressure and Range

WIKA's introduction to DP measurement notes that in almost every DP application the working pressure is much higher than the DP range, and the maximum working pressure is very critical. Midwest protects its bellows and Bourdon units against overrange in either direction up to the rated working pressure, with a relief valve that opens to equalise the two sides.

At a glance

Low end: Ranges go as low as 0.2 inches of water for air handling, and WIKA lists models from 2.5 mbar.
High end: WIKA says a Bourdon type DP gauge can work at up to 15,000 psi.
Piston leakage: Midwest states piston bypass will not exceed 15 SCFH of air at 100 PSID.
Weight: Some DP gauges weigh up to 30 lbs (13.6 kg), so plan the mounting bracket.

Did you know?

WIKA gives a simple example: 10 inches of water is 0.36 psi. Two standard gauges reading about 100 psi could not show that difference, but one DP gauge can.

Applications

Filters and strainers: DP rises as the element clogs and tells you when to change it. WIKA describes filter monitoring on reverse osmosis membranes with microswitch alarms that still work in a power failure.
Flow: An orifice, venturi or nozzle creates a DP; WIKA notes the drop is proportional to the square of the flow rate. See our types of flow meters guide.
Level: On an open tank the low side vents to atmosphere. On a sealed tank the high side goes to the bottom and the low side to the top, so the gas pressure cancels.
Other duties: NOSHOK lists pumps, separators, leak detection and heat exchangers; Midwest adds HVAC and irrigation filtration.

For level work the hydrostatic pressure calculator gives the DP for a liquid height, and the DP flow square root calculator converts a reading to flow. More background is in the Instrumentation Blog pressure section.

Selection Checklist

  1. DP range. Size the dial for the difference you expect, not the line pressure.
  2. Maximum working pressure. Confirm the rating covers the highest line pressure and any one sided overpressure.
  3. Fluid. Piston for liquids; diaphragm for gas, mixed or dirty media; bellows or Bourdon for best accuracy.
  4. Wetted materials. Corrosive media may need stainless steel. Ask about NACE or oxygen service if required.
  5. Connections. WIKA says filters typically use inline end connections, low air pressures use hose barbs, and level often uses four ports with 1/4 inch NPT female threads.
  6. Options. Switches, output signals and a valve manifold with shutoff and an equalising bypass. See manifolds and fittings.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Choosing by DP range alone. The working pressure rating usually decides the design.
A piston gauge on gas. The bypass between ports can be too large; use an isolated diaphragm design.
Adding diaphragm seals without checking. Midwest does not recommend seals on several of its models and says fitting them voids the warranty.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a differential pressure gauge measure?

The difference between the pressure at its high port and its low port, shown on one dial.

Which design suits gas?

A diaphragm type, because its ports are fully isolated. Piston types allow a small bypass that can be too large for gas.

Why is working pressure so important?

The ports often see a line pressure far above the DP range, and the gauge must withstand it on either side.
