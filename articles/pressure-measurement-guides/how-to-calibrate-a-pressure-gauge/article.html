Key takeaways

  • Use a reference at least 4 times more accurate than the gauge you are checking, the traditional 4:1 test uncertainty ratio.
  • Exercise the gauge, then read at least 3 to 5 points going up and the same points coming down, so hysteresis shows.
  • Work the error out as a percent of span and compare it with the ASME B40.100 grade: a Grade 1A gauge must stay within ±1% of span.
  • Correct for any height difference when the test medium is a liquid. One metre of water is about 98 mbar.

Pressure gauge calibration means comparing the gauge with a more accurate reference at known pressures, recording the error, and adjusting the movement if the error is outside the gauge's accuracy grade. SWME supplies pressure gauges and the test and calibration equipment used to check them, including hand pumps and reference gauges.

Reference Equipment and the 4:1 Rule

Fluke's comparison of deadweight testers and pressure comparators describes the two common setups. A deadweight tester generates, controls and measures pressure from force over area, and the pressure is typically known to 0.025% or better, with 0.015% models available. Its masses must be adjusted for local gravity if the tester moves to another site, and loading them is slow and can be done wrong.

A comparator has two ports, one for the reference gauge and one for the device under test. It is easier to use and usually cheaper, but less accurate than a deadweight tester, and the pressure needs time to settle after each change. Either way, Fluke gives the traditional requirement as a 4:1 test uncertainty ratio. For example, its 700G digital reference gauge is rated 0.05% of scale and the 2700G 0.02% of scale, so both are good references for a 1% gauge. The same check applies if one of SWME's digital pressure gauges is used as the reference.

Deadweight tester

  • Pressure from known masses on a known piston area
  • Typically 0.025% or better
  • Masses corrected for local gravity
  • Slower, and masses can be loaded wrongly

Comparator with reference gauge

  • Two ports: reference and gauge under test
  • Easier to use, usually cheaper
  • Accuracy set by the reference gauge
  • Let pressure settle after each change

Accuracy Grades

You cannot call a gauge in or out of tolerance without its grade. Ashcroft's page on ASME accuracy grades for mechanical gauges lists the B40.100 grades, all as a percent of span. The last column is our arithmetic for a 0 to 10 bar gauge.

ASME B40.100 gradeAccuracy, % of spanOn a 0 to 10 bar gauge
4A±0.1% over the full dial±0.01 bar
3A±0.25% over the full dial±0.025 bar
2A±0.5% over the full dial±0.05 bar
1A±1% over the full dial±0.1 bar
A±1% in the middle, ±2% at the ends±0.1 bar middle, ±0.2 bar ends
BBetween ±2% and ±3%±0.2 to ±0.3 bar

Beamex adds that B40.100 classes run from 0.1 to 5% and EN 837 classes from 0.1 to 4%, so a class 1 gauge of 0 to 100 psi is good to ±1 psi. Our guide on how to select a pressure gauge covers choosing the grade in the first place.

Pressure Gauge Calibration Step by Step

The setup below is a comparator with the reference and the gauge under test on one manifold. Beamex's list of pressure gauge calibration tips and Reotemp's gauge calibration procedure give the sequence.

{{infographic}}
  1. Mount the gauge as it is used. Usually vertical. Beamex says a 5 degree change in position should not shift the reading by more than half the accuracy class.
  2. Leak test. Pressurise, let the pressure stabilise, and watch for a drop before you take readings.
  3. Exercise the movement. Apply the maximum pressure for a minute, vent and wait a minute, two or three times.
  4. Read going up. Reotemp applies 25, 50, 75 and 100%. Adjust the pressure until the pointer sits exactly on a mark, look straight at the dial, and tap the case lightly to settle the pointer before you read the reference.
  5. Read coming down. Reduce pressure slowly and check 75, 50 and 25%. With a hand pump, do not overshoot a point and come back, or the hysteresis result is lost.
  6. Record and decide. Note every error as found, adjust if needed, then repeat the full run and record the results as left.

The difference between the up and down readings at one point is the hysteresis. Beamex's note on hysteresis in pressure calibration gives an example: 49.95 kPa rising and 50.05 kPa falling at the 50 kPa point, which is 0.10 kPa of hysteresis.

Adjusting Zero, Span and Linearity

Reotemp's procedure gives the adjustments for a Bourdon tube movement, in this order:

Adjustments on a mechanical gauge (Reotemp)

Zero: If the gauge has a stop pin, set the pointer at 10% with 10% pressure applied. With no stop pin, set the pointer at zero with no pressure.
Span: Move the full scale slide. If the reference reads higher than the gauge, reduce the distance between pushrod and shaft; if it reads lower, increase it.
Linearity: Adjust the pushrod length. If the reference reads higher than the gauge, lengthen the pushrod; if lower, shorten it. Repeat until every point is within limits.

Worked Example

Take a 0 to 10 bar Grade 1A gauge, so the tolerance is ±0.1 bar. At a reference of 5.00 bar it reads 5.06 bar going up. The error is 0.06 bar, which is 0.6% of span and uses 60% of the tolerance, so this point passes. On the way down it reads 5.09 bar, so the hysteresis at that point is 0.03 bar, or 0.3% of span. Test Medium and Head Correction

Beamex says low pressure gauges are practical to calibrate with air or gas, while for higher pressure a liquid is more practical and safer. With a liquid, any height difference between the reference and the gauge adds a pressure of ρgh. For water, a 1 m difference causes an error of 9.8 kPa, which is 98 mbar or 1.42 psi.

Did you know?

On a 0 to 10 bar Grade 1A gauge the whole tolerance is ±0.1 bar, or 100 mbar. A 1 m height difference with water, at 98 mbar, would use almost all of it, so put the reference and the gauge at the same height or correct the readings.

Calibration Interval

Ashcroft's note on pressure gauge calibration intervals suggests a check once every 12 months, with shorter intervals for high vibration, pulsation or rapid cycling. The end user sets the interval, not a standards body. Recalibrate at once if the pointer is not at zero or sits outside the zero box.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Reading only on the way up. Without the descending points you cannot see hysteresis.
Using a reference that is barely better than the gauge. Keep to at least 4:1, for example a 0.05% reference for a 1% gauge.
Ignoring the height of a liquid column. One metre of water adds about 98 mbar.
Recording only the final result. Keep the readings as found and as left, so you can see drift over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many points should I test?

Additel says 3 to 5 points are typically taken going up and then coming down. Reotemp uses 25, 50, 75 and 100% up and 75, 50 and 25% down.

How accurate must the reference be?

Traditionally at least 4 times more accurate than the gauge under test, the 4:1 test uncertainty ratio.

How often should a pressure gauge be calibrated?

Ashcroft suggests every 12 months, and more often with vibration, pulsation or rapid cycling. You set the interval for your plant.
